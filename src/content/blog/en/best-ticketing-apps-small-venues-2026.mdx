<h1>Best ticketing apps for<br/><em>small venues</em> in 2026</h1>

<p class="article-intro">Choosing a ticketing platform is one of the most consequential decisions a small venue makes. Get it wrong and you're locked into a contract, handing over 10% of every sale, or stuck with software that takes 30 minutes to create a simple event. This is an honest comparison of the main options — including their real pricing, what they're actually built for, and where each falls short for independent venues.</p>

<div class="article-body">

## What small venues actually need

Before comparing platforms, it's worth being clear about what "small venue" ticketing requirements actually look like. Most bars, pubs, clubs, and independent event organizers need:

- Fast event creation — you're running a business, not managing a software project
- Mobile door scanning — a phone, not a scanner gun or iPad station
- Clean fan checkout — no surprise fees that make your fans feel ripped off
- Attendee data — email addresses you actually own
- Reasonable pricing — ideally under €60/month or a fair per-ticket rate

None of these are exotic requirements. But not all platforms deliver on all of them.

## The platforms compared

<div class="platform-card">
  <div class="platform-name">Eventbrite</div>
  <span class="platform-tag">Market leader · Built for large events</span>
  <p>Eventbrite is the household name in ticketing and it shows — in both its strengths and its pricing. The platform is polished, widely recognised by fans, and has decent discovery features for major events. But for a 150-cap venue running weekly nights, the fees are punishing: up to 8% service fee + 2.9% + €0.79 payment processing on the standard plan. For a €15 ticket, that's nearly €1.90 per ticket gone before you see a penny.</p>
  <p>Eventbrite also owns your attendee data more than you might expect — exporting emails requires navigating several menus, and their marketing push can mean your fans get Eventbrite promotions alongside your confirmation emails.</p>
  <p class="platform-verdict">Verdict: Good for one-off large events where discovery matters. Poor value for recurring venue nights.</p>
</div>

<div class="platform-card">
  <div class="platform-name">Dice</div>
  <span class="platform-tag">Music-focused · Curation-led</span>
  <p>Dice is genuinely good if you're running a music venue with the right profile — it has a strong reputation in the electronic and indie music world, a slick fan-facing app, and a resale system that keeps ticket prices honest. The platform takes no upfront fee from venues (they charge fans a small booking fee directly), and there's real discovery potential if your events fit their curated feed.</p>
  <p>The catch: Dice is selective about which venues they work with. Getting approved takes time, and if your venue runs a mix of event types (quiz nights, comedy, club nights, live music), you may not fit their model. The platform is also less flexible for organizers who want full control over their data and messaging.</p>
  <p class="platform-verdict">Verdict: Excellent for the right music venue. Too niche and selective for general-purpose event organizers.</p>
</div>

<div class="platform-card">
  <div class="platform-name">TicketTailor</div>
  <span class="platform-tag">Affordable · UK-based · Good for recurring events</span>
  <p>TicketTailor is one of the most popular alternatives to Eventbrite for UK and Irish independent venues. It operates on a credit-based model — you buy a bundle of credits (roughly €0.35–€0.65 per ticket depending on volume) rather than paying a percentage. For low-priced events this is significantly cheaper than Eventbrite's percentage fees.</p>
  <p>The platform is well-designed, reliable, and supports most of what a small venue needs: customizable event pages, mobile scanning, attendee exports, and integrations with Mailchimp and Zapier. The main limitation is that the door-scanning app is basic, and there's no built-in communication tool for emailing ticket holders directly from the platform.</p>
  <p class="platform-verdict">Verdict: Strong choice for UK and Irish venues running regular events where per-ticket fees would bite. Less polished than newer alternatives.</p>
</div>

<div class="platform-card">
  <div class="platform-name">Evntry</div>
  <span class="platform-tag">Built for bars, pubs &amp; clubs · Zero fan fees</span>
  <p>Evntry was built from the ground up for exactly this use case — the 50–300 capacity venue running regular events that wants a simple, fast ticketing setup without the overhead of enterprise platforms. Event creation takes under 5 minutes. The free plan charges a 3% transaction fee (you choose whether to absorb it or pass it on); the custom Pro plan removes all transaction fees for unlimited events.</p>
  <p>Fans pay no booking fee at checkout — the price they see is the price they pay. The door-scanning app works on any smartphone, attendee data is fully exportable at any time, and there's a built-in email tool for messaging your ticket holders before or after an event. The trade-off: no marketplace discovery (your events don't appear in a "find events near me" feed), which means your promotion drives all your ticket sales.</p>
  <p class="platform-verdict">Verdict: Best fit for venues that own their audience and want a lean, affordable setup with no surprise fees for fans.</p>
</div>

- **Best door scanning:** Evntry, Eventbrite

## The honest answer

No single platform is objectively best for every venue. The right choice depends on your volume, your event type, and how much you're willing to pay per ticket versus per month.

If you run less than 2 events per month and want to avoid any monthly commitment, TicketTailor's credit model or Evntry's free plan are the most sensible starting points. If you run 3 or more events monthly and your average ticket price is over €10, a flat-fee subscription almost always beats percentage fees within a few months.
