<h1>How to sell tickets online<br/>with <em>zero fees</em></h1>

<p class="article-intro">Most ticketing platforms quietly take 10–15% of every ticket you sell — fees that get passed to your fans or swallowed by your margin. There's a better way. This guide explains how venue owners, bar managers, and independent event organizers can sell tickets online without giving away a slice of every sale.</p>

<div class="article-body">

## Why ticketing fees are a bigger problem than they look

Let's say you put on a gig and sell 200 tickets at €15 each. That's €3,000 in revenue. A platform charging a 12.5% booking fee takes €375 — before you've paid the band, the bar staff, or the venue hire. Scale that across 20 events a year and you've handed over €7,500 to a platform that had nothing to do with making the night happen.

The pain is shared by your fans too. Many platforms add their fee on top of the ticket price as a "service fee" or "booking fee," meaning a €15 ticket becomes a €17.50 surprise at checkout. Fans notice. **It erodes trust in your brand, not theirs.**

## The two fee models you'll encounter

When you're comparing ticketing platforms, fees come in two flavours:

- **Percentage-of-sale fees** — the platform takes a cut of every ticket (typically 5–15%). The bigger the ticket price, the more they take.
- **Flat booking fees** — a fixed amount per ticket (e.g. €1.50 per ticket), regardless of price. This tends to hurt lower-priced events more.

Some platforms combine both. And many add payment processing on top of their own cut — so the headline "3% fee" can quietly become 6–8% once Stripe's margin is included.

## How zero-fee ticketing actually works

Zero fees for fans doesn't necessarily mean free for you. Here's how the math typically works on platforms designed for independent venues:

- **Subscription model** — you pay a flat monthly or annual fee, and in return you pay 0% per ticket. For venues running regular events, this almost always works out cheaper than per-ticket fees.
- **Venue absorbs the fee** — some platforms give you the option to pay the processing fee yourself rather than passing it to fans. The fan sees a clean ticket price; you cover a small processing cost.
- **Truly free tier** — a small number of platforms offer a free plan for low-volume sellers with a modest percentage fee, graduating to fee-free once you upgrade. This is ideal if you're just starting out.

## What to look for in a zero-fee ticketing platform

Fee structure is just one piece. Here's what else matters when you're choosing a platform for your venue:

- **Setup speed** — if it takes more than 10 minutes to create your first event, it's not built for busy venue managers. Look for platforms where you can go from idea to live event page in under 5 minutes.
- **Door scanning** — a platform that doesn't offer mobile QR scanning is half a product. You need to be able to scan tickets from your phone without special hardware.
- **Attendee data ownership** — you should be able to export a full list of ticket buyers at any time. Your fans are your fans. A platform that won't give you their email addresses is holding your audience hostage.
- **No lock-in** — can you cancel at any time? Can you export everything? If the answer to either is "no," that's a red flag.
- **Communication tools** — the ability to email your ticket holders before or after the event is worth more than most venue owners realise. It's how you build a fanbase, not just sell tickets.

## The maths: subscription vs. per-ticket

Here's a simple comparison. You run a 100-capacity venue with an average of 3 events per month, average ticket price €12, and 70% capacity per event (70 tickets sold per event).

- Monthly ticket revenue: 3 events × 70 tickets × €12 = **€2,520**
- At 10% booking fee: **€252/month** paid to the platform (€3,024/year)
- At 5% fee: **€126/month** (€1,512/year)
- At 3% fee: **€75.60/month** (€907/year)

The savings add up quickly — and your fans see a cleaner checkout, which improves conversion.

## Step-by-step: switching to zero-fee ticketing

1. **Audit your current spend** — log into your existing platform and look at the total fees paid over the last 3 months. Multiply by 4 for an annual estimate. That's your baseline.
2. **Pick a platform with a free trial or free tier** — test the event creation flow, the scanning app, and the checkout experience before committing.
3. **Create one event and run it in parallel** — if you're switching mid-calendar, run your new platform alongside the old one for one event. Get comfortable with the checkout and scanning before fully migrating.
4. **Update your links** — anywhere you've shared your old ticketing page (Instagram bio, website, email newsletters) should point to the new platform.
5. **Tell your fans** — a simple post explaining that you've moved to a platform with no booking fees is a positive story. It frames the switch as you doing something for them.

## Common objections — answered

**"My fans are used to buying from [big platform]."** Fans follow events, not platforms. As long as the checkout is mobile-friendly and the confirmation email arrives promptly, they won't care who's running the back end.

**"Big platforms have discovery built in."** For independent venues, discovery via ticketing marketplaces is close to zero — almost all your ticket sales come from your own promotion (Instagram, email, flyers, word of mouth). The platform's discovery engine is rarely the reason a fan comes to your event.

**"Switching is too much hassle."** Setting up a new event on a modern platform takes 5–10 minutes. The admin is a one-time cost; the savings on fees are permanent.
