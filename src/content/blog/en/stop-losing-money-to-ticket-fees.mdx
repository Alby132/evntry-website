Most event ticketing software charges you twice. First a percentage of the ticket price, then a flat fee on top, stacked per ticket. On a €15 ticket, that combination commonly reaches double digits once you add it up, and the flat portion doesn't scale down. It hits a €10 ticket exactly as hard as a €50 one.

It doesn't matter whether you or your fans end up paying that fee. Either way it comes out of the same night's revenue. So before picking event ticketing software, it's worth working out where that money actually goes.

## Where traditional platforms take their cut

The standard model on most ticketing platforms: a percentage per ticket, plus a flat charge, usually passed to the person buying the ticket at checkout. It shows up as a line item fans see right before they pay, and it's a big part of why checkout abandonment is such a common complaint among venues using these platforms.

The flat fee is the real problem for smaller venues. A percentage scales with price. A flat fee doesn't. Run a €10 door ticket through a typical platform's fee structure and the flat charge alone can eat a meaningful chunk of it, on top of the percentage. Low ticket prices — exactly what most 50–500 capacity bars, clubs, and event spaces charge — get hit hardest.

## What Evntry does differently

Fans pay zero booking fees on Evntry. What you set as the ticket price is what they pay. No surcharge added at checkout, no second number that appears right before they commit to buying. That part doesn't change no matter what happens on the venue side.

The venue side isn't free. We're not going to pretend it is. Evntry runs on a subscription starting at €99/month, sized to your venue, plus 3% per ticket sold. That's the honest structure: a predictable monthly cost plus a small percentage, instead of a stacked percentage-and-flat-fee combination that scales worse the more tickets you sell and the cheaper those tickets are.

The subscription is sized to your venue rather than one-size-fits-all, because a 50-capacity bar running occasional events and a 400-capacity club running weekly shows don't have the same volume or the same cost tolerance.

## Why fans not paying a fee matters to you too

This isn't just a fan-friendliness angle. A checkout that adds an unexpected fee at the last step is a checkout people abandon. Every fan who closes the tab because the total jumped past what they expected is a ticket you didn't sell. Removing that fee removes that friction. It shows up as more completed purchases, not just goodwill.

It also changes how your ticket price reads when you promote an event. "€15" means €15 everywhere it's posted: Instagram bio, a shared link, a poster. No asterisk, no "plus fees" fine print undercutting the number you actually want people to see.

## Who this fits best

This makes the most sense for venues running events regularly — weekly or several times a month. The subscription is a fixed monthly cost, so the more you sell against it, the more it works in your favor. A venue doing four sold-out nights a month gets far more value out of a flat subscription than one running a single event every couple of months.

If you're only running the occasional one-off event, a monthly subscription isn't the right fit yet. That's exactly what a free tier is for. No need to commit to a subscription until you're actually running things on a regular schedule.

## The bottom line

Traditional ticketing platforms take a cut that combines a percentage and a flat fee, and that combination commonly reaches double digits, hitting lower-priced tickets the hardest. Evntry keeps the fan side untouched: zero booking fees, full stop. And it's upfront about the venue side: a subscription starting at €99/month plus 3% per ticket.

For venues doing enough volume to make it count, that's less coming off the top than the stacked-fee model most platforms run on. Not a zero-fee promise across the board. Just a more honest one, and a smaller one, where it matters.
