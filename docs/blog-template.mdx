This is the lead paragraph. It gets a larger font treatment automatically — no heading needed before it. Write 2–3 sentences that hook the reader and frame what the article is about. State the problem or tension clearly.

A short second paragraph is fine if the intro needs a little more setup. Keep it tight — the first H2 section should start within the first few sentences.

## First Section Heading

Body paragraphs go here. Each paragraph is plain text — no heading prefix. Aim for 2–4 paragraphs per section, each making one clear point.

**Bold text** is used for key terms, numbers, or phrases worth emphasising. Use it sparingly — a few times per article at most.

## Second Section Heading

Another section. Keep each one focused on a single idea. If you find yourself covering two topics in one section, split it into two.

- Bullet lists work well for comparisons, step-by-step instructions, or feature summaries.
- Keep list items parallel in structure.
- Three to six items is usually the right range.

## Third Section Heading

This is where specifics go — numbers, examples, concrete scenarios. The more concrete the better.

> A blockquote can highlight a key insight or a particularly important sentence that deserves to stand apart from the body text.

## Fourth Section Heading (optional)

Add more sections as needed. Three to five H2 sections is the target range. More than five usually means the article can be tightened or split.

## The Bottom Line

A short closing section that lands the main point without repeating the whole article. One or two paragraphs. End on something actionable or honest — what should the reader do next, or what's the realistic expectation?

## FAQ

### Does Evntry [specific feature or claim]?

Answer directly. One to three sentences. If the answer is nuanced, acknowledge the nuance rather than oversimplifying.

### How does [pricing / setup / something the reader will wonder about] work?

Explain it plainly. Avoid jargon. If there's a catch or caveat, state it — candour builds trust more than a polished non-answer.

### Is Evntry the right fit if [common edge case]?

Be honest. If the answer is "probably not yet", say so. Point toward the free tier or a realistic evaluation instead of pushing a sale that isn't the right fit.

### What happens if [common concern]?

Address the concern directly. Reassure where you genuinely can; redirect where you can't.
